Life in Sesto San Giovanni follows the rhythm of a normal residential neighbourhood rather than a tourist destination.

You'll find people stopping for breakfast before work, shopping at local supermarkets, meeting friends for an aperitivo and using public transport as part of their daily routine. ## Everyday essentials

One of the biggest advantages of staying in Sesto San Giovanni is having everyday services close at hand.

### Supermarkets

You'll find several supermarkets nearby, including **Esselunga**, **Coop**, **Lidl** and **Carrefour**, making it easy to shop for groceries or pick up everyday essentials during your stay.

### Pharmacies

Pharmacies are conveniently located throughout the neighbourhood should you need medicines or personal care products.

### Local market

Every **Saturday**, Via **Puricelli Guerra** hosts the local street market.

It's a popular place to buy fresh fruit and vegetables, clothing and household goods while experiencing everyday neighbourhood life beyond the main tourist areas.

## Food & coffee

You don't need to travel into central Milan for a good meal. Sesto San Giovanni offers plenty of everyday options within walking distance, whether you're looking for breakfast, a quick lunch or a relaxed evening out.

### Breakfast & coffee

The neighbourhood has several cafés and pastry shops where locals stop before work. Whether you prefer a quick espresso at the counter or a cappuccino with a fresh pastry, you'll find plenty of choice nearby.

### Casual meals & restaurants

For lunch or dinner, you'll find bakeries, sandwich shops, pizzerias and traditional Italian restaurants alongside a selection of international cuisine, making it easy to eat well without leaving the neighbourhood.

### Aperitivo & evening options

Several bars around Sesto Rondò offer a relaxed aperitivo atmosphere with drinks and light snacks. It's a pleasant way to end the day and experience the neighbourhood's quieter evening rhythm.

## Safety

### Is Sesto San Giovanni safe?

For most visitors, Sesto San Giovanni feels like a typical residential town where people commute, shop and go about their daily lives.

As with any urban area, it's sensible to take normal precautions, particularly around busy transport hubs or late at night. During the day, the main streets and areas around the metro stations are generally busy with local residents.

Keeping an eye on your belongings and staying aware of your surroundings is usually all that's needed for a comfortable visit.

Yes. The M1 Red Line provides direct access to central Milan, while regional trains make it easy to reach destinations beyond the city.

### Are there supermarkets nearby?

Yes. Several supermarkets, including Esselunga, Coop, Lidl and Carrefour, are located throughout the neighbourhood.

### Where can I have breakfast?

There are numerous cafés and pastry shops across Sesto San Giovanni, making it easy to enjoy a traditional Italian breakfast close to your accommodation.

### Can I find restaurants without going into Milan?

Yes. Sesto San Giovanni offers a good selection of cafés, pizzerias, Italian restaurants and international cuisine within walking distance.

### Good to know

Most shops, supermarkets and cafés follow regular opening hours, although some independent businesses may close for part of the afternoon or on certain days of the week.

Sesto San Giovanni has its own tourist tax, which is separate from the City of Milan's tourist tax. Rates and exemptions may change over time, so it's always worth checking the latest information before your stay.

Public transport services, travel times and routes may vary because of maintenance works, strikes or special events. Before travelling, check the latest information from **ATM Milano** and the relevant railway operator if you're following a fixed schedule.
