Travelling with children changes what matters when choosing accommodation.

For most families, a smooth daily routine is more valuable than staying within walking distance of a famous landmark. Good transport connections, everyday services, quieter surroundings and enough space often make a bigger difference than a central postcode.

## What families should look for

Before choosing a neighbourhood, think about what will make each day easier rather than what looks best on a map.

Look for accommodation with:

- a metro station within walking distance;
- supermarkets and pharmacies nearby;
- restaurants suitable for families;
- quiet residential surroundings;
- enough space for everyone;
- a fully equipped kitchen if you're staying several days.

These practical features usually have a greater impact on family travel than staying closer to the city centre.

## Choosing the right area

Different neighbourhoods suit different types of family trips.

### Historic centre

Best if your priority is walking to Milan's main attractions, although accommodation is often smaller, busier and more expensive.

### Brera

A charming district with restaurants, galleries and elegant streets, but generally one of the city's most expensive areas.

### Porta Nuova

A modern neighbourhood with good transport connections, pedestrian spaces and nearby parks, making it a practical option for shorter stays.

### Residential neighbourhoods

Families staying several days often appreciate quieter streets, more spacious accommodation and better access to everyday services than the busiest tourist districts.

### Sesto San Giovanni

Sesto San Giovanni is a good example of this balance. It combines a residential atmosphere with direct metro and railway connections, allowing families to reach central Milan easily while returning to a quieter area in the evening.

If you're considering this option, our guide to [Is Sesto San Giovanni a Good Place to Stay When Visiting Milan?](/guides/is-sesto-san-giovanni-a-good-place-to-stay) explores the area in more detail.

## Getting around with children

Once you've chosen a well-connected area, exploring Milan without a car becomes much easier.

Families usually benefit most from direct journeys that avoid unnecessary changes, especially when travelling with young children or strollers.

One of the best examples is the **M1 Red Line**, which provides straightforward access to many of Milan's most popular destinations.

Choosing a well-connected area can also make it easier to explore beyond the city during your stay.

- **Lake Como**, for a relaxing day by the lake;
- **Lecco**, combining lakeside walks with mountain scenery.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Milan a good destination for families?

Yes. Milan combines parks, museums, pedestrian areas and an efficient public transport network, making it an easy city to explore with children.

### Is it worth staying outside the historic centre?

Often, yes. A well-connected residential neighbourhood can offer more space, quieter evenings and better value while keeping the city's main attractions easy to reach.

### How important is being near a metro station?

Very important. Easy access to the metro usually saves more time and effort than staying slightly closer to one attraction.

### Is Sesto San Giovanni suitable for families?

For many families, yes. It offers a good balance of residential comfort, everyday services and direct public transport connections.

### Is an apartment better than a hotel for families?

For longer stays, many families appreciate having a kitchen, more living space and the flexibility that an apartment provides.
