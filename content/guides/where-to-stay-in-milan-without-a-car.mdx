Exploring Milan without a car is not only possible—it is often the easiest way to experience the city.

An extensive network of metro lines, trams, buses and regional trains connects the main attractions, meaning your choice of accommodation matters more than having a parking space. The key is to stay somewhere that makes moving around simple.

For a practical introduction to the network, read our guide on [how to use Milan public transport](/guides/how-to-use-milan-public-transport).

## What really matters when choosing accommodation

The best area isn't necessarily the closest to the city centre—it's the one that makes daily travel easy.

When comparing neighbourhoods, consider:

- a metro station within walking distance;
- direct connections to the places you plan to visit;
- as few transport changes as possible;
- supermarkets, cafés and other everyday services;
- railway access if you're planning trips beyond Milan.

These factors usually have a bigger impact on your stay than the postcode itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a car to visit Milan?

No. Most visitors can comfortably explore Milan using the metro, trams, buses and regional trains.

### Is it better to stay in the city centre?

Not necessarily. A well-connected neighbourhood with direct public transport often makes travelling around Milan easier than a central location with poorer connections.

### Which metro line is best for tourists?

The **M1 Red Line** is one of the most convenient because it connects Duomo, San Babila, Cadorna and Rho Fiera Milano.

### Is Sesto San Giovanni a good place to stay without a car?

Yes. It combines direct metro access, railway connections and the everyday services most travellers need.

### Can I visit Lake Como by train?

Yes. Several towns around Lake Como can be reached by regional train, making them ideal day trips without renting a car.
