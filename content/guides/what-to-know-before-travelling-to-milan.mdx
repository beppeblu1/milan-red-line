Travelling to Milan is straightforward, but a few practical details are worth checking before you leave home.

Electrical plugs, card payments, mobile data, drinking water and everyday travel habits may work differently from what you are used to. Knowing the basics in advance can save time after arrival and help you avoid unnecessary purchases or last-minute problems.

This guide focuses on the practical things international visitors should know before travelling to Milan and Italy.

It is not a packing list. Instead, it covers the small pieces of information that are useful to understand before your trip begins.

## Who is this guide for?

This guide is useful if you are:

- visiting Milan or Italy for the first time;
- travelling from outside Italy;
- unsure whether your electrical devices will work;
- wondering how widely cards are accepted;
- deciding what to do about mobile data;
- checking practical everyday differences before departure;
- preparing for your arrival in Milan.

## Milan travel basics at a glance

Before travelling, remember these essentials:

- Italy uses the **euro (€)**;
- the electricity supply is **230 V / 50 Hz**;
- Italian sockets commonly include **Type C and Type L**, with other compatible or multistandard sockets also frequently encountered;
- cards and contactless payments are widely useful, but carrying a small amount of cash is sensible;
- EU/EEA travellers can generally use their mobile plan under EU roaming rules, subject to their provider's conditions;
- travellers from outside the EU/EEA should check roaming costs before departure;
- tap water in Milan is drinkable;
- official entry requirements should always be checked with the relevant authorities for your nationality and circumstances.

The most useful preparation is not buying more things. It is checking what you already have and understanding what will work when you arrive.

## Electrical plugs and voltage in Italy

One of the easiest things to check before travelling is whether your chargers and electrical devices are compatible with Italy.

### What voltage does Italy use?

Italy uses an electricity supply of approximately:

- **230 volts**
- **50 Hz**

Look at the label on your charger or power supply.

Many modern chargers for:

- smartphones;
- laptops;
- tablets;
- cameras;
- USB chargers;

are designed for a wide input range such as:

> 100–240 V, 50/60 Hz

If your charger shows a range like this, it normally supports the Italian electricity supply and you may only need a plug adapter.

### Which plugs are used in Italy?

Visitors commonly encounter:

- **Type C**, with two round pins;
- **Type L**, the traditional Italian three-pin arrangement;
- multistandard sockets capable of accepting more than one compatible plug format.

Depending on your country of origin, you may therefore need a travel adapter.

### Adapter or voltage converter?

These are not the same thing.

A **plug adapter** changes the physical shape of the plug so that it fits the socket.

A **voltage converter** changes the electrical voltage supplied to a device.

If your device supports 230 V or has an input range such as 100–240 V, a voltage converter is generally unnecessary.

If it does not support the Italian voltage, do not assume that a simple plug adapter makes it safe to use.

Check the manufacturer's specifications before connecting the device.

## Paying in Milan

Italy uses the **euro (€)**.

For most visitors, the easiest approach is to travel with:

- at least one payment card;
- a backup payment method;
- a modest amount of cash.

### Cards and contactless payments

Card and contactless payments are widely used in Milan, particularly at:

- hotels and accommodation;
- restaurants;
- supermarkets;
- shops;
- museums and attractions;
- transport-related services.

Mobile wallets can also be convenient where contactless payments are accepted.

However, relying on a single card is unnecessary risk when travelling.

Your card may occasionally be:

- declined by your bank;
- temporarily blocked;
- incompatible with a particular terminal;
- lost or damaged.

A second payment method gives you a simple backup.

### Should you carry cash?

You do not need to carry large amounts of cash for an ordinary stay in Milan.

A small amount can still be useful for:

- minor purchases;
- small businesses;
- situations where a payment terminal is temporarily unavailable;
- unexpected circumstances.

Avoid carrying more cash than you reasonably expect to need.

### Check your bank before travelling

Before departure, check:

- foreign transaction fees;
- ATM withdrawal fees;
- exchange-rate policies;
- whether your bank needs to know that you are travelling;
- how to freeze or replace your card if it is lost.

These details depend on your bank and card provider rather than on Milan itself.

## Phones, roaming and mobile data

Your best mobile-data option depends largely on where your existing phone plan was issued.

### Travelling from the EU or EEA

If your mobile plan is from an EU or EEA country, EU roaming rules generally allow you to use calls, texts and mobile data while travelling in Italy under conditions similar to those at home.

Fair-use policies and specific limits can still apply.

Check your provider's current roaming conditions before travelling.

### Travelling from outside the EU or EEA

Do not assume that your normal mobile plan will be inexpensive in Italy.

International roaming can vary substantially between providers.

Before departure, compare:

- your operator's roaming package;
- a travel eSIM;
- an Italian or European SIM/eSIM where appropriate.

The purpose here is not to identify a universally cheapest option. The important step is to understand your existing plan before your phone connects to an Italian mobile network.

### Check whether your phone supports eSIM

If you are considering an eSIM, verify before travelling:

- whether your phone supports eSIM;
- whether it is carrier-unlocked where necessary;
- whether you can install the eSIM before departure;
- which SIM will handle calls, messages and mobile data.

Doing this before the trip is usually easier than troubleshooting connectivity immediately after landing.

## Drinking water in Milan

Tap water in Milan is safe to drink and is regularly monitored.

For most visitors, this means there is no need to rely exclusively on bottled water during the stay.

You can:

- drink tap water at your accommodation;
- refill a reusable bottle;
- use Milan's public drinking fountains where appropriate.

### Milan's public fountains

Around Milan you may notice small green drinking fountains commonly known as **vedovelle**.

They provide drinking water and are a familiar part of the city's streetscape.

A reusable water bottle can therefore be useful when exploring the city, particularly during warmer months.

## Entry requirements and travel documents

Entry requirements depend on factors such as:

- your nationality;
- where you are travelling from;
- the length of your stay;
- the purpose of your visit.

Because these requirements can change and depend on individual circumstances, do not rely on a general Milan travel guide for visa or immigration advice.

Before travelling, verify the current requirements through the appropriate official authorities.

Also check:

- the validity required for your passport or identity document;
- whether a visa is required;
- any documentation required for minors;
- the conditions that apply to your specific trip.

Keep important travel documents accessible during your journey rather than placing everything in checked luggage.

## Medicines and prescriptions

If you travel with medication, keep essential medicines accessible during the journey.

For prescription medicines, it can be useful to travel with:

- the medication in its original packaging;
- the prescription or supporting medical documentation where appropriate;
- enough medication for the planned stay, with a reasonable margin for disruption.

Rules can differ depending on the medicine and the country from which you are travelling.

If you carry controlled or unusual medication, check the applicable official requirements before departure.

## Everyday practical differences

Some differences are small enough that they rarely cause serious problems, but knowing about them makes the first few hours easier.

### Language

Italian is the local language.

In central Milan, major attractions, hotels and businesses serving international visitors, English is commonly encountered.

However, you should not assume that everyone you meet will speak English.

Learning a few basic Italian expressions can be useful.

### Time

Italy uses Central European Time and observes seasonal daylight-saving changes.

If you are travelling across several time zones, check the local time when arranging:

- accommodation check-in;
- airport transfers;
- restaurant reservations;
- trains;
- timed attraction tickets.

### Emergency number

The European emergency number is **112**.

It can be used for genuine emergencies requiring assistance from emergency services.

## Frequently asked questions

### What plug adapter do I need for Milan?

Italy commonly uses Type C and Type L sockets, alongside multistandard sockets in many buildings. The adapter you need depends on the plug used in your home country.

Also check that your electrical device supports Italy's 230 V / 50 Hz electricity supply.

### Do I need a voltage converter in Italy?

Not necessarily.

Many modern phone, laptop and camera chargers accept 100–240 V and therefore work with the Italian voltage when used with the appropriate plug connection.

Always check the input specification printed on your device or charger.

### Can I pay by card in Milan?

Cards and contactless payments are widely useful in Milan.

It is still sensible to have a backup payment method and a small amount of cash.

### What currency does Milan use?

Milan uses the euro (€), like the rest of Italy.

### Will my phone work in Milan?

Usually yes, provided that your phone and mobile plan support roaming in Italy.

EU/EEA travellers generally benefit from EU roaming rules. Visitors from elsewhere should check their provider's roaming charges before travelling.

### Should I buy a SIM or eSIM for Italy?

It depends on your existing mobile plan.

If international roaming is expensive, a travel or local SIM/eSIM may be worth considering. Check your current provider first rather than buying another plan automatically.

### Can you drink tap water in Milan?

Yes. Milan's tap water is drinkable and regularly monitored.

Public drinking fountains are also available around the city.

### Do I need cash in Milan?

You can complete many everyday transactions by card, but carrying a small amount of cash provides a useful backup.

### Do I need a visa to visit Milan?

Visa and entry requirements depend on your nationality and circumstances.

Check the current requirements with the appropriate official authorities before travelling.

### What should I pack for Milan?

What you should pack depends heavily on the season, weather and purpose of your trip.

This guide intentionally focuses on practical travel basics rather than packing. A dedicated Milan packing guide should cover clothing, footwear, seasonal weather and what to bring.
