The Milan Metro is one of the easiest ways to move around the city, even if you have never used it before.

Each line has a number, a colour and one or more final destinations. Once you understand how directions and interchange signs work, most journeys become straightforward.

This guide focuses only on using the Metro: recognising lines, choosing the correct platform, reading station signs and changing trains.

For tickets, fares and contactless payment, read our dedicated guide to [Milan Public Transport Tickets](/guides/milan-public-transport-tickets).

## Who is this guide for?

This guide is useful if you are:

- visiting Milan for the first time;
- unfamiliar with underground transport systems;
- unsure how to choose the correct direction;
- planning journeys that require a change of line;
- staying outside the historic centre and relying on the Metro.

## How the Milan Metro is organised

Milan has five Metro lines:

- **M1 Red Line**
- **M2 Green Line**
- **M3 Yellow Line**
- **M4 Blue Line**
- **M5 Purple Line**

The letter and number identify the line, while the colour makes it easier to recognise on maps and station signs.

Several stations connect two or more lines. These are interchange stations, where you can change trains without leaving the Metro system.

The five lines serve different parts of Milan, but you do not need to memorise the complete network. For most journeys, you only need to know:

1. your starting station;
2. the line you need;
3. the terminus that indicates your direction;
4. the station where you leave or change.

### The five lines at a glance

The **M1 Red Line** crosses central Milan and serves destinations including Duomo, Cadorna, Rho Fiera Milano and Sesto San Giovanni.

The **M2 Green Line** serves Milano Centrale, Cadorna, Garibaldi and several eastern and southern areas.

The **M3 Yellow Line** runs north to south through stations including Centrale and Duomo.

The **M4 Blue Line** connects Linate Airport with central and western Milan.

The **M5 Purple Line** serves northern and western areas, including Bicocca, Garibaldi and San Siro.

This overview is enough to recognise the network. Your journey planner or Metro map will identify the specific line and interchange you need.

## Understanding directions and termini

Metro platforms are normally identified by the final destination, or **terminus**, of the train.

This is the most important concept for first-time users.

Imagine that your journey uses the M1 and your destination lies in the direction of Sesto 1° Maggio FS. Inside the station, follow signs showing that terminus. For a journey in the opposite direction, follow the relevant western terminus instead.

Some lines divide into branches. In those cases, two trains may initially travel along the same route before separating.

Before boarding, check:

- the destination shown on the platform signs;
- the destination displayed on the front or side of the train;
- whether your station lies on that branch.

Milan Metro signs generally use termini rather than north, south, east or west. Match your journey with the final destination shown on the map.

### A simple direction check

Before going through the station, answer these questions:

1. Which line do I need?
2. What colour is it?
3. Which terminus is in my direction?
4. Do I need to change line?
5. At which station do I leave?

Keeping these five answers in mind prevents most mistakes.

## Reading station signs

Metro stations use consistent signs to direct passengers towards:

- individual lines;
- platforms and termini;
- interchange routes;
- street exits;
- lifts and accessible routes;
- local transport connections.

Follow the coloured line symbol and the name of the terminus you need.

Large interchange stations may have long corridors. Continue following the same line colour and destination rather than assuming the nearest platform is correct.

### Platform displays

Electronic displays generally show the destination of the next train and the waiting time.

On lines with branches, confirm the train's destination before boarding. A train can be travelling in the correct general direction but continue along a different branch after the shared section.

### Finding the correct exit

Large stations often have several exits leading to different streets.

Before leaving the platform area, look for:

- the street name;
- a nearby landmark;
- the exit number or letter where provided;
- signs for connecting services.

Checking the most convenient exit in your journey planner can save a long walk at street level.

## Changing lines

Interchange stations are marked on the Metro map where two or more coloured lines meet.

To change line:

1. leave the train at the interchange station;
2. follow signs for the colour and number of the next line;
3. identify the correct terminus for the next part of the journey;
4. continue to the corresponding platform.

You normally remain inside the Metro system during a standard interchange.

Do not follow the exit signs unless your route specifically requires you to leave the station.

### Allow time at larger interchanges

Changing trains may take several minutes, especially at large or complex stations.

Stations such as Duomo, Cadorna, Centrale and Garibaldi connect important routes and can be busy. Follow signs calmly and allow other passengers to move past before stopping to check your map.

## Using the Metro map

The official map shows:

- all five Metro lines;
- every station;
- interchange points;
- connections with suburban and railway services;
- accessibility information where available.

Start by locating your departure and arrival stations.

Then trace the simplest route between them. If they are on different lines, identify the station where the colours meet.

For most visitors, the easiest approach is to use a reliable journey planner for the route and the Metro map to understand it.

### A practical example

A journey from Sesto San Giovanni to Duomo uses the **M1 Red Line**.

Both locations are on the same line, so no interchange is required. You only need to:

- enter the M1 station;
- choose the direction towards the appropriate western terminus;
- remain on the train until Duomo.

This is why accommodation with a direct Metro connection can simplify daily travel without requiring detailed knowledge of the entire network.

For more context about this route, see [Where to Stay Near the M1 Red Line](/guides/where-to-stay-near-the-m1-red-line).

## Frequency, operating hours and practical tips

Metro trains generally run frequently during the day, so most visitors do not need to plan around a precise timetable.

Waiting times can be longer:

- early in the morning;
- late in the evening;
- on Sundays and public holidays;
- during maintenance or service disruption.

Operating hours and final departures vary by line, station and day. Check the official ATM app or journey planner when travelling very early or late.

After Metro services finish, replacement night buses operate on several routes. These belong to the wider public transport network rather than the Metro itself.

For an overview of Metro, tram and bus travel together, read [How to Use Milan Public Transport](/guides/how-to-use-milan-public-transport).

### Accessibility

Accessibility differs between stations and may also depend on the direction of travel.

Maps indicate lifts and stairlifts, but equipment can temporarily be unavailable. Travellers who need a step-free route should check the real-time status of station facilities through ATM before leaving.

The newer M4 and M5 lines were designed with modern accessibility features, while accessibility on older lines can vary by station.

### Practical tips for your first journey

- Prepare the route before entering the station.
- Take a screenshot if your mobile connection may be unreliable underground.
- Keep away from the platform edge while checking directions.
- Let passengers leave the train before boarding.
- Move away from doors after entering.
- Keep bags close in crowded stations and trains.
- Recheck the terminus after every interchange.
- Do not rely only on the colour: also confirm the line number and destination.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Milan Metro easy to use?

Yes. The network is clearly colour-coded and station signs consistently identify lines, termini and interchanges. The main skill is learning to choose direction by terminus.

### How do I know which direction to take?

Find your destination on the Metro map and identify the terminus beyond it. Follow station signs displaying that terminus.

### How do I change Metro lines?

Leave the train at an interchange station and follow signs for the colour and number of the next line. Then choose the platform using the terminus for your new direction.

### What happens if I take the Metro in the wrong direction?

Leave at the next convenient station, follow signs for the opposite platform and travel back. Check whether changing direction requires stairs, corridors or a different platform area.

### How often do Metro trains run?

Services are generally frequent during the day, but intervals vary by line, time and day. Check ATM information when travelling early, late or during service changes.

### Is the Milan Metro accessible?

Many stations provide lifts or stairlifts, but accessibility varies and equipment can be temporarily unavailable. Check the real-time status of the stations needed for your journey.

### Do I need to understand the full Metro map?

No. You normally need only your line, direction, interchange and destination. A journey planner can calculate the route while the map helps you understand it.
