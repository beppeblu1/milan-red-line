Milano Centrale is one of Italy's busiest railway stations and the main gateway for many visitors arriving in Milan by train.

Although the station is large, most journeys follow a simple pattern. Once you understand how the platforms, central hall and transport connections fit together, moving through the station becomes much easier.

This guide explains how to use Milano Centrale efficiently, whether you are arriving in the city, changing trains or continuing your journey by Metro, taxi or airport bus.

It does not explain how to buy train tickets or how to use the railway system itself. Those topics belong to dedicated guides.

## Who is this guide for?

This guide is useful if you are:

- arriving in Milan by train;
- leaving Milan from Milano Centrale;
- changing to another train;
- looking for the Metro after arriving;
- catching an airport bus;
- visiting Milano Centrale for the first time.

## Milano Centrale at a glance

Milano Centrale is Milan's main railway station and one of the country's most important transport hubs.

It connects:

- high-speed trains;
- regional services;
- international routes;
- the M2 Green Line;
- the M3 Yellow Line;
- airport buses;
- taxis;
- local buses and trams.

You do not need to memorise the station layout before arriving.

Instead, remember a simple principle:

1. arrive at your platform;
2. follow signs towards the main hall;
3. continue following signs for your next connection.

This approach works for almost every journey.

### Understanding the station layout

The platforms occupy one side of the station.

After leaving your train, passengers naturally move towards the large central concourse.

From there, clearly marked signs guide travellers towards:

- Metro stations;
- exits;
- taxis;
- airport buses;
- luggage storage;
- information points;
- other station services.

Rather than thinking of Milano Centrale as a maze, imagine it as a central hub where almost every connection begins from the main hall.

## Arriving at Milano Centrale

Most visitors experience the station in exactly the same way.

After the train stops:

- leave the platform;
- follow the flow of passengers;
- continue towards the station building;
- ignore shops until you know where you need to go.

Large overhead signs make navigation straightforward.

If your next step is already planned, simply keep following the appropriate symbols rather than stopping to study the entire station.

### Changing to another train

If you have a connecting train, first check:

- the departure platform;
- the departure time;
- whether the platform has already been announced.

Electronic departure boards throughout the station display the latest information.

If your platform has not yet been assigned, wait near the departure boards rather than walking unnecessarily through the station.

{/* rx:transport */}

## Finding your platform

If you are departing from Milano Centrale, arrive with enough time to locate your platform before boarding begins.

Departure boards show:

- train number;
- destination;
- scheduled departure time;
- assigned platform.

Most platforms are clearly numbered and easy to reach from the central hall.

When the platform is announced, simply follow the overhead signs.

There is usually no need to rush.

Boarding normally begins several minutes before departure.

### Before boarding

Take a moment to confirm:

- the train number;
- the destination;
- your carriage, if indicated on your ticket.

Checking these details before boarding is easier than correcting a mistake after departure.

## Moving from the platforms to the main hall

Almost every journey through Milano Centrale passes through the station's main hall.

After leaving your train, simply follow the signs towards the station exits. The passenger flow naturally leads from the platforms into the central concourse, where most onward connections begin.

Avoid stopping immediately at the end of the platform if you are unsure where to go. Moving into the main hall first gives you access to better signs, information boards and station services.

If you need to meet someone, the main hall is also much easier to recognise than the platform area.

## Finding the Metro

Milano Centrale is served directly by two Metro lines:

- **M2 Green Line**
- **M3 Yellow Line**

Both are clearly signposted from inside the station.

Follow the Metro symbols together with the colour of the line you need. The walk is straightforward, although it can take several minutes because of the size of the station.

You do not need to leave the station area and search outside. Simply continue following the official Metro signs.

### Reaching the M1 Red Line

Milano Centrale is **not** served directly by the M1 Red Line.

If your destination lies on the M1, first take either:

- the **M2 Green Line**, or
- the **M3 Yellow Line**,

then change at one of the appropriate interchange stations.

For a practical explanation of Metro lines, directions and interchanges, read our guide to [How to Use the Milan Metro](/guides/how-to-use-the-milan-metro).

## Finding taxis

Official taxis are available outside Milano Centrale.

After leaving the station, follow the signs for the taxi rank rather than accepting offers from people approaching travellers inside or immediately outside the station.

During busy periods you may find a queue, but official taxi ranks are organised and clearly marked.

If you prefer to book a ride through an app, move away from the busiest pedestrian areas before requesting your vehicle.

## Finding airport buses

Several airport bus companies operate services from the area around Milano Centrale.

Follow the signs for airport buses after leaving the station.

Different operators may use different stopping points, so always check your ticket or the operator's instructions before walking to the departure area.

Avoid assuming that every airport bus leaves from the same stop.

## Useful station services

Milano Centrale offers the essential facilities most travellers need before continuing their journey.

These include:

- luggage storage;
- information points;
- ticket offices;
- waiting areas;
- toilets;
- food and convenience shops.

Because shops and services may change over time, rely on the station signs rather than memorising specific locations.

### Luggage storage

If you have several hours before your next train or want to explore Milan without carrying bags, luggage storage is available inside the station.

Opening hours and prices may change, so check the current information after arriving.

## Practical tips

A few simple habits make using Milano Centrale much easier.

- Check your next connection before leaving the platform.
- Follow the official station signs instead of other passengers.
- Allow extra time if you need to reach the Metro or airport buses.
- Keep your ticket and phone easily accessible.
- If your departure platform has not yet been announced, wait near the departure boards.
- Stay aware of your belongings in busy areas.
- If in doubt, ask at an official information point rather than relying on unofficial advice.

## Safety and common mistakes

Milano Centrale is busy throughout the day, especially during commuter hours and holiday periods.

Like any major transport hub, it is sensible to remain aware of your surroundings and keep personal belongings close.

The most common mistakes made by first-time visitors include:

- following the crowd instead of the signs;
- arriving late for a connection;
- confusing Metro entrances with station exits;
- walking to the wrong airport bus stop;
- accepting transport offers from unofficial drivers.

Most of these problems can be avoided simply by allowing enough time and following the official station signage.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Milano Centrale easy to navigate?

Yes. Although the station is large, the main passenger routes are clearly signposted and most journeys follow the same pattern from the platforms to the main hall.

### How long does it take to reach the Metro?

The walk usually takes several minutes, depending on your arrival platform and the number of passengers.

### Which Metro lines serve Milano Centrale?

Milano Centrale is served directly by the M2 Green Line and the M3 Yellow Line.

### Can I reach the M1 directly from Milano Centrale?

No. You need to change from either the M2 or the M3 at an interchange station.

### Where can I leave my luggage?

Luggage storage is available inside the station for travellers who need to store bags before continuing their journey.

### Where do airport buses leave from?

Airport buses depart from the area outside the station. Always check the exact departure point with your bus operator.

### Is Milano Centrale safe?

Yes. It is a busy transport hub used by thousands of passengers every day. As in any large station, remain aware of your belongings and use official transport services.

### How early should I arrive for a connecting train?

Allow enough time to leave your arrival platform, reach the departure boards, locate your next platform and board comfortably, especially during busy periods.

### Good to know

Milano Centrale is not the only major railway station in Milan.

Before travelling, always check that your train departs from **Milano Centrale** and not from another station such as **Milano Porta Garibaldi**, **Milano Cadorna** or **Milano Rogoredo**.

If you are continuing by Metro, remember that Milano Centrale connects directly only with the **M2 Green Line** and **M3 Yellow Line**. Journeys on the **M1 Red Line** require an interchange.

Station services, accessibility features and transport connections may occasionally change. Before travelling, check the official information provided by the station, your train operator or ATM if you need the latest updates.
