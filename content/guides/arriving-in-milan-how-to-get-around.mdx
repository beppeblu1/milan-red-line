## Milan's main arrival points

Most visitors arrive through one of four transport hubs:

- **Malpensa Airport**, Milan's largest international airport;
- **Linate Airport**, the closest airport to the city;
- **Orio al Serio Airport**, popular with low-cost airlines;
- **Milano Centrale**, the city's main railway station.

Each arrival point offers different transport options, but all connect efficiently with the city's public transport network.

The best connection depends on your arrival point, travel time, luggage and final destination.

## How do you continue your journey?

After arriving, your next step depends on where you're staying.

Some visitors continue by metro, others by train, bus or airport shuttle. Understanding your arrival hub makes choosing accommodation much easier, especially if you'll be travelling across the city during your stay.

## Why transport matters when choosing where to stay?

For most visitors, accommodation should be chosen according to how easily it connects with the places they'll visit.

Areas served by the **M1 Red Line**, for example, provide direct access to many of Milan's main attractions, business districts and exhibition venues without complicated journeys.

Rather than focusing only on distance from the city centre, consider how simple your daily travel will be.

If you're comparing accommodation along the metro, our guide to [Where to Stay Near the M1 Red Line](/guides/where-to-stay-near-the-m1-red-line) explores the different options in more detail.

For visitors travelling without a car, our guide to [Where to Stay in Milan Without a Car](/guides/where-to-stay-in-milan-without-a-car) explains the main factors to consider.

## Choosing a practical base

Good accommodation is about more than being close to the city centre.

Once you know where you're arriving and how you'll continue your journey, compare possible areas according to:

- how easily you can reach the places you'll visit most often;
- whether you'll rely mainly on the metro or regional trains;
- the balance between convenience, value and daily travel time;
- access to everyday services during your stay.

These factors often matter more than distance from a particular landmark.

Many visitors therefore prefer well-connected residential areas that offer reliable transport, better value and useful local services.

## Local tip

> Don't choose your accommodation based only on the airport you're arriving at.

Most visitors spend far more time travelling around Milan than travelling from the airport. A well-connected area along the metro network will usually make the rest of your stay much easier.


{/* rx:day-trips */}

## Arriving from Milan's airports

Each airport connects to the city in a different way, so it's worth checking the most convenient option before you travel.

- **Malpensa Airport** is linked to Milan by the Malpensa Express train and airport coaches.
- **Linate Airport** connects directly to the M4 Blue Line and several bus services.
- **Orio al Serio Airport** is served mainly by dedicated airport coaches to Milano Centrale.

Before travelling, compare the latest journey times, operating hours and ticket options using the official airport and transport websites.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I book airport transfers before arriving in Milan?

In most cases, no. Milan's airports and railway stations offer frequent public transport connections, and tickets can usually be purchased on arrival. Booking in advance is mainly useful if you have specific travel requirements or arrive very late at night.

### Is public transport available for late-evening arrivals?

Many airport and city transport services operate until late, but schedules vary depending on the route and the day of the week. If you're arriving late at night, check the final connection before travelling and keep an alternative option in mind.
